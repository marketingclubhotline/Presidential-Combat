COMPETITIVE FAIRNESS

Every leader has a fighting chance. Our goal is for skill, timing, strategy and practice—not a fighter's country or political identity—to decide the winner.

How we balance moves

High damage must have a meaningful trade-off. A powerful move may require a longer startup, more recovery after a miss, shorter range, meter expenditure, a stricter input, or an opening for the opponent to counter. Difficult inputs alone do not justify an uncounterable move.

FactorWhat it means
Damage / chipHealth lost on a clean hit / while blocking
StartupFrames before the attack becomes active
Active framesFrames during which the attack can connect
RecoveryFrames before the attacker can safely act again
Range / speedHow far and how quickly an attack can reach
Meter / riskResources spent and vulnerability if blocked or missed
CounterplayWhether opponents can block, evade, interrupt or punish it

Same rules, different personalities

National symbols, comedy, voice lines and animations do not automatically make a fighter stronger. Comparable attacks should offer comparable value; fighters may differ in offense, defense, reach and mobility, but each must have practical winning strategies.

How we check fairness

We compare damage, frame data, resource cost, risk and counterplay; then test both sides of each matchup, monitor results across skill levels, review player feedback, and adjust outliers. A 50/50 win rate alone does not prove balance.

This is our balancing policy and development target, not a claim that all matchups have already been proven fair. This prototype is still being tested. Published move values and balance changes will be documented as the roster expands.
